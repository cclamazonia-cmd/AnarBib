# Guide — Subject indexing in AnarBib

> **Who this guide is for.** For any comrade who catalogues and wants books to be
> found by **topic** — not only by title or authorship. Subject indexing means
> leaving trails for whoever comes looking.
>
> **Spirit.** There is no single "right" way. The thesaurus is a **commons**:
> it grows with what we propose, it is not a grid imposed from above. If something
> is missing, propose it; if doubt persists, free keywords are always there.
> Nothing here locks you in, nothing here grades you.

## The two fields

The cataloguing form has **two** subject fields, side by side — by design:

1. **Subjects (authority · matter)** — the **controlled thesaurus**. You search for
   an existing term and add it; it is shared across the whole network and is
   multilingual. It is what feeds the facets and topic navigation in the public
   catalogue.
2. **Keywords** — **free text**, specific to this record. No governance, no
   friction: type whatever you like, separated by « ; ». It is the release valve —
   for when no thesaurus term fits, or you do not want to wait.

> Simple rule: **prefer the thesaurus** (better discoverability, coherence across
> the network); use **keywords** for what is too specific or does not exist yet.

## How to choose a subject

- **Search before creating.** Start typing in the « Subjects » field: the thesaurus
  suggests existing terms, with their hierarchy (« Anarcho-communism ·
  Anarchism »). Reusing terms avoids duplicates.
- **Fair granularity.** Not too broad (« Politics »), not too narrow
  (« The strike of such-and-such year in such-and-such city »). Think of the term
  someone would use to **look for** this book.
- **Multiple subjects are fine.** A book can have 2 to 4 subjects. There is no
  need to be exhaustive — the main ones are enough.
- **The scope note** (when it exists) appears beneath the term: it says how to use
  it. Read it if in doubt.

## When to propose a new term

If **no** existing term fits, create one right there (« + create … »). It is born
as **proposed**: you can use it immediately, but it is **not yet official**.

- The **cataloguing coordination** reviews proposed terms in the **Matter** tab and
  **activates** them (or discontinues duplicates). The default leans toward « yes,
  and »: integrate and tidy up, rather than a flat « no ».
- A proposed term shows a « proposed » label in the picker. This is normal — it
  is not an error, it is the commons growing.

## The « missing label » flag (⚐)

If a subject has no label **in your language**, the picker shows the term as a
fallback (usually in pt-BR) with a ⚐. This is not a bug: it is an invitation.
The label in your language can be **completed** — each language is written by its
own community, not by top-down translation.

## The spirit: reach out, do not grab the wrist

- The thesaurus **suggests**, it does not impose. You are never stuck: free
  keywords are always available.
- Proposing a term is **your** gesture; activating it is the coordination's
  gesture. Nobody polices, nobody punishes.
- The goal is not a perfect classification, but that **books find each other**
  — and that the vocabulary of the commons grows with care, at the point of use.

> Part of the network's **knowledge commons** (see the relational charter
> « the outstretched hand » and the framing document « mutual aid in cataloguing »).
> Other languages are built in parallel, by language community.
